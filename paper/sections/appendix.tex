\section{Experimental Setup and Additional Results}
\label{app:setup}

\subsection{Models and serving}
All agents and generators run locally via vLLM 0.31.0 (OpenAI-compatible
API, \texttt{--enforce-eager}, temperature 0 for agents and the judge,
temperature 0.7 with a fixed seed for the generator) on RTX 4090 GPUs (24\,GB): Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct serves as the primary agent backbone and as
the plausibility judge; Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct serves as the contamination
generator and as the secondary agent backbone. Qwen2.5-3B/1.5B are used in
the scale ablation. Every LLM call is logged with its token counts; agents
cap episodes at ten steps, forced-choice probes at six.

\subsection{Official artifacts}
We use WebShop's official \texttt{items\_shuffle.json} (1{,}181{,}430
products after key cleaning and deduplication), \texttt{items\_ins\_v2.json},
and \texttt{items\_human\_ins.json} (10{,}136 human instructions), with the
benchmark's own loader, goal builder, and reward function applied
unmodified; the only semantic addition is best-variant scoring of the
chosen listing (\S\ref{sec:method-env}).

\subsection{Contamination pool}
The pool covers 100 tasks (the official goal list under seed 2027), three
families, four listings per family per task (up to 1{,}200 before judge
drops; 1{,}089 survive in the released pool), with a
plausibility floor at judge score~4 (regenerate once, then drop). Synthetic
economics are assigned programmatically: price = gold price $\times$
$\mathrm{U}(0.75,0.95)$ (deterministic per listing), rating
$\mathrm{U}(4.6,4.9)$, review counts in $[400,3000)$, and reviews in packs
of 20 (reviews family) or 6 (others). An archived pre-fix pool with a
\$50 price anchor is released as an ablation.

\subsection{Gate outcomes}
\label{app:gate}
P0: success $0.65$ at $\varepsilon=0$, zero malformed actions, 18/20
tasks exactly reproducible under greedy decoding (two flips from
continuous-batching numerics). P1 (final, corrected pool): $\sigma=0.377$
$[0.263,0.493]$ over 300 duels (task-cluster bootstrap CI); per family:
clone $0.523$ $[0.366,0.688]$, geo $0.351$ $[0.206,0.495]$,
reviews $0.287$ $[0.156,0.441]$. Reported honestly: the pre-registered gate
($\sigma\ge0.60$ overall) \emph{failed} --- agents on average
\emph{reject} machine-optimized pages --- but the strong family
differentiation (clones at chance, gloss rejected) met the gate's intent in modified form, redirecting the study toward
channel-specific economic lures; all later measurements were
pre-registered against this revised premise (original gate text
preserved in the repository). P2 (gate run, 40 paired tasks):
success $0.525\to0.450$, regret $0.338\to0.398$ at $\varepsilon=0.75$
versus $0$. The main dose--response curve ($N{=}100$ per dose):
success $0.590\to0.360$ (McNemar $p=1.3\times10^{-4}$), regret
$0.262\to0.443$ (paired $\Delta=+0.181$, CI $[+0.095,+0.264]$),
confidence $0.822\to0.875$, ECE $0.249\to0.496$; P4E replicates the
endpoints under a second contamination seed (pooled McNemar
$p=1.2\times10^{-8}$). Two earlier P1/P2 runs were invalidated by a
rendering defect and a price-anchoring bug; both are documented with
their data in the repository.

\subsection{Power and multiple comparisons}
\label{app:power}
The paired regret difference at $\varepsilon=0.75$ has task-level SD
$\approx0.43$; at $N=100$ per dose the endpoint McNemar test detects
the observed success drop ($p=1.3\times10^{-4}$; pooled over two
seeds, $p=1.2\times10^{-8}$), and the paired regret difference has a
bootstrap CI excluding zero. Intermediate doses are reported as
effect sizes with cluster-bootstrap CIs; interpretation rests on the
joint pattern across outcomes and its replication across families,
backbones, scales, and defenses.

\paragraph{Family-wise correction.} Applying Holm--Bonferroni across the
eleven confirmatory tests leaves the two headline dose contrasts
unambiguous (pooled success: adjusted $p=1.3\times10^{-7}$; full-parity
probe: $1.0\times10^{-3}$) and the $10\%$ threshold ($p=0.023$); the
remaining channel comparisons (price parity, rating parity,
provenance-vs-none, stealth recovery, the $s{=}25\%$ envelope point)
survive uncorrected but not Holm-corrected (adjusted
$p\in[0.072,0.078]$). \textbf{$N{=}100$ defense battery}
(Figure~\ref{fig:replication}b): re-run on independent second-generation
pools at two-and-a-half-fold sample size, the arms reproduce the
ordering --- provenance $+0.098$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.021,+0.175]$)
and structure $+0.102$ ($[+0.027,+0.176]$) recover mean reward,
spoof-25\% $+0.153$ ($[+0.077,+0.230]$) and spoof-50\% $+0.079$
($[+0.002,+0.154]$) show the forgery-tolerance gradient, the PPR
variants stay at or below zero, text prompts hurt ($-0.161$, CI
$[-0.241,-0.080]$), reviewtrust is indistinguishable from no defense,
and the random placebo sits at $+0.000$; sign-flip permutation
$p$-values (coarse at $n{=}100$) leave no arm Holm-significant. We
therefore read the decomposition as a convergent joint pattern --- two
channels of nearly equal magnitude, an additive account, and an
independent threshold law reaching the same conclusion --- not as a
collection of individually significant contrasts.

\paragraph{Cluster robustness.} Probe duels are clustered by task (30
clusters; the 88 clone duels span 27); all probe intervals are
task-level cluster bootstrap CIs. Cluster-robust re-testing of the
pre-registered contrasts leaves the joint pattern intact: full-parity
(drop $0.273$, CI $[+0.176,+0.390]$, $p<0.001$) and the 10\% threshold
(gain $0.205$, CI $[+0.050,+0.365]$, $p=0.011$) remain reliable; the
overall preference for real listings weakens but holds ($\sigma=0.377$,
$p=0.036$); price parity is directional only (drop $0.136$, CI
$[-0.023,+0.300]$, $p=0.097$). Duel-level McNemar $p$-values are
retained as the pre-registered tests, and the Holm analysis above uses
them unchanged.

\paragraph{Equivalence at saturation.} The pricing-threshold claim that
``deeper discounts add nothing'' is supported by two-proportion tests
against the $10\%$ point ($20\%$: $p=0.45$; $30\%$: $p=0.76$; $20\%$ vs.\
$30\%$: $p=0.65$; $n{=}88$ per arm). These are non-significance results with modest power; we phrase the
claim as saturation of the observed response, not as a strict
equivalence bound.

\paragraph{Structural detector vs.\ oracle.} With $n{=}40$ paired tasks,
the minimal detectable regret difference at two-sided $\alpha=0.05$,
$80\%$ power is roughly $0.11$, larger than the observed oracle--struct
gap ($0.171$ vs.\ $0.250$). We conclude only that structural filtering
recovers at least the full regret excess ($0.398\to0.250$, below the
uncontaminated $0.338$), not equality with the oracle.

\subsection{Real-world attack-surface mining}
\label{sec:app-real}
Section~\ref{sec:res-realworld} grounds the tested discount regime in
real catalogs. This appendix documents the two sources and a text
provenance check.

\paragraph{Sources and pairing rules.}
\emph{WebMall}~\cite{peeters2025webmall}:
offers grouped across four shops by exact normalized name (244 groups,
505 offers; GTIN empty in the distribution). \emph{WebShop}~\cite{yao2022webshop}:
80{,}000 items blocked by brand token and category; title-token
Jaccard in $[0.80,0.95)$ with distinct ASINs marks lookalikes,
$\geq0.95$ near-identical (232 pairs). Close non-matches are the
real-world clone analogue; identical-name cross-shop groups are the
near-identical control, where buying cheaper is correct (per-source
values in \texttt{realdata/real\_stats.json}).

\paragraph{Headline mining numbers.} WebMall cross-shop groups: 53.3\% of 244 (median
11.1\%). WebShop 80{,}000-item sample: 497 lookalike pairs (Jaccard
$0.80$--$0.95$) at 83.3\% (median 21.0\%), versus 51.7\% for 232
near-identical pairs ($\geq0.95$).

\paragraph{Can provenance be detected from text alone?}
GPT-2 perplexity~\cite{radford2019gpt2}, calibrated on 250
human-written WebShop descriptions, separates our pools (AUC $0.912$;
median perplexity $\approx22$ synthetic vs.\ $\approx55$ human). First,
susceptibility is a \emph{decision-level} failure --- the agent buys
listings it could in principle distinguish --- so structural
provenance guarantees, not better perception, are the operative
defense (Section~\ref{sec:res-defense}). Second, we do \emph{not}
estimate the share of AI-generated text in real catalogs: applying
the threshold to WebMall text flags a large fraction, but the
estimate is dominated by genre and length confounds
(93.6\%$\to$55.3\% under length matching alone), and no ground
truth exists for real-offer provenance; the multi-shop benchmarks we
build on~\cite{peeters2025webmall} likewise treat provenance as
unknown.

\paragraph{Real-document protocols (RA1--RA3).}
\emph{RA1 (clone).} 467 lookalike pairs (Jaccard $\in[0.80,0.95)$,
natural discount $\geq10\%$, instruction filter applied here but not
in the 80k census above) with two candidates per task (gold,
real lookalike) under official semantics; clone price natural or
$f\in\{0.9,0.8\}$ of gold. Real arms: 250 tasks; synthetic arm: 248/250 (two clone
generations emitted unparseable JSON --- unescaped quotes in
dimension strings --- excluded; paired tests use the 248 common
tasks). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct: $\sigma=0.616/0.636/0.600$
($p{=}2.4\times10^{-4}$, $1.7\times10^{-5}$, $1.6\times10^{-3}$);
our generator's clone wins $0.677$ on the same tasks (McNemar
$p{=}0.167$): not generator-specific at this sample size; deeper
discounts add no significant gain ($0.636$ at $f{=}0.8$ vs.\ $0.600$ at
the natural price; paired McNemar $p{=}0.093$, $n{=}250$).
\emph{RA3 (reviews).} Real same-category
listings carry Yelp fraud-filtered spam reviews (label $=-1$, rating
$\geq4$) with the same programmatic inflation as the synthetic
family; $\varepsilon{=}0.75$, $n{=}100$: regret $0.327$, success80
$0.550$ (synthetic family $0.454/0.450$, $n{=}40$; paired vs.\ clean on the
40 common tasks: 21 non-tied pairs, 11 vs.\ 10, $p{=}1.0$).

\subsection{Backbone generality and scale}
\label{sec:app-backbone}
The dose--response replicates on Llama-3.1-8B
(success $0.475\to0.300$ at $\varepsilon=0.75$; regret $0.346\to0.502$,
$+45\%$; synthetic purchases 55\%). Across scales, smaller agents are
\emph{at least} as susceptible --- Qwen2.5-3B buys synthetic listings in
70\% of tasks (vs.\ 54\% for 7B) and its regret rises by a further 27\%
in relative terms --- but the success-level dose contrast requires
minimal baseline competence, which Qwen2.5-1.5B lacks (success $0.300$ at
$\varepsilon=0$, statistically unchanged; clean regret already $0.578$,
a floor effect). Summarizing the two competent backbones by a linear
erosion coefficient $\kappa$ (regret increase per unit contamination,
endpoint regression): $0.242$ (95\% CI $[0.129,0.352]$) for 7B and
$0.208$ (95\% CI $[0.037,0.374]$) for 8B --- overlapping intervals, the
same order of vulnerability.

\subsection{Washout experiment (M1)}
\label{app:m1}
The experiment is pre-registered in the repository (written before
any M1 data was collected). Five arms: \textbf{B0}, 20 clean episodes with the same memory apparatus
(equal-experience control); \textbf{M0}, 20 exposure episodes at
$\varepsilon{=}0.75$ mix (six synthetic listings per page, same gated
generator); \textbf{M2}, exposure with notes written but never injected
back; \textbf{M3}, exposure plus an explicit corrective entry after every
synthetic purchase; \textbf{M4}, starts from M0's end-of-exposure memory
with all entries containing pre-registered price words deleted
(18-word list in the preregistration); M4 skips the parity exam. Memory
injection is recency-based (last three entries); lessons are distilled
every five episodes by the same model. Exposure tasks are official goals
$[100{:}120)$; exams use lookalike pairs disjoint from every other
experiment --- discounted ($f{=}0.9$) pairs $[250{:}290)$ and parity
($f{=}1.0$) pairs $[290{:}330)$, 40 pairs each with fixed clone
instances across arms and checkpoints. Exam pairs never appear in any previously reported experiment (RA1
uses $[0{:}250)$) and exposure draws on goals $[100{:}120)$, so the
exam is leak-free by construction. Eight clone-generation failures
leave $n{=}39$ (exam) and $n{=}38$ (parity), fixed across all arms.
Checkpoints are clean episodes $\{0,2,\ldots,20\}$. Seeds: exposure
$50000{+}e$,
clean $60000{+}e$, exam shuffles $41000{+}p$, generation $99000{+}g$.

A shared no-memory pre-exam gives $\sigma=0.692$, and the B0 arm tracks
it ($0.718$ at episode 2, $p{=}0.80$): the memory apparatus itself is
neutral on the clean path. Pre-registered main tests: the
internalization hypothesis on the discounted exam fails ($0.667$ vs.\
$0.718$, $p{=}0.62$); the partial-decay branch holds (checkpoint-0
carryover McNemar $9/1$, $p{=}0.021$; half-life 4 clean episodes);
memory causality holds (write-only arm at chance, $0.692$ vs.\ $0.897$,
$z{=}-2.24$, $p{=}0.025$). The parity persistence ($0.842$ vs.\
$0.579$, $p{=}0.011$) and its memory mediation ($0.790$ vs.\ $0.526$,
$p{=}0.016$) are pre-registered secondary tests (single-seed
findings; see below). The transfer matrix at
checkpoint 0 is exploratory ($n{=}20$): same-category pairs show no
elevation ($0.718$ vs.\ $0.744$, $p{=}0.80$); a cross-category transfer
was \emph{not executable} (remaining pool pairs all lie inside the five
exposure categories --- a feasibility limitation); the cross-domain
praise-block test gives $1.00$ vs.\ $0.70$ ($p{=}0.008$), but the
write-only ($0.85$) and surgery ($1.00$) arms are also elevated ---
exploratory with mixed evidence. The reward autopsy
computes, for every exposure episode, the counterfactual official reward
of the gold product: 38 synthetic purchases, 2 with reward at or above
gold, mean gap $-0.632$.

\paragraph{Pre-registered re-randomizations (seeds 2028, 2029).}
Two replication runs shift the treatment-side randomness (exposure
pools, episode dynamics, memory trajectories; trajectories in
Figure~\ref{fig:replication}a) while re-running the same five arms; in
seed-2029 the exam instrument --- instruction
texts, clone candidates, and presentation order --- is byte-identical
to seed-2027, which restores the pre-exposure baseline ($0.718$ vs.\
seed-2027's $0.692$; the fully shifted seed-2028 raises it to $0.821$
because goal-pool shuffling changes 24 of 40 exam instructions).
Pre-registered verdicts: the internalization failure replicates in
both runs ($p{=}0.53$, $p{=}0.62$); the fast-channel carryover is
direction-consistent ($+3$ and $+5$\,pp) but not significant
($p{=}1.0$, $p{=}0.75$); the parity persistence and the write-only
mediation contrast do not replicate ($p{\ge}0.46$) and are reported
as single-seed findings in the main text; trajectories, pools, and
verdict logs are released.
