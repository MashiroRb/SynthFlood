\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Preference, not perception.}
Our own first reading of the probe --- the one a trajectory skim produces
--- was that agents ``cannot tell'' clones from real products. The controlled decomposition falsifies that
reading. At price parity the clone is \emph{rejected} ($\sigma=0.386$); at
rating parity it is rejected identically ($0.386$); at both, it is
rejected almost $3{:}1$ ($0.250$) --- and the two channels decompose
additively, each worth roughly $14$ percentage points. The dangerous form
is therefore not the attention-grabbing page
nor the perceptually perfect forgery, but the \emph{economically
attractive counterfeit}: a listing that copies a real product's
specifications and undercuts it with discount and manufactured social
proof. The pricing dose--response sharpens this into a threshold law ---
a $10\%$ discount restores majority-level preference, deeper discounts
saturate ---
which means the attack requires no optimization of the forgery itself,
only access to the pricing surface. This recasts the threat model:
attacks target the agent's \emph{preference alignment} (what it trades
off), not its perception (what it can detect), and defenses that shore up
detection leave the actual channel open. The structural filter did in fact
drive regret below the uncontaminated level; our $n{=}40$ design cannot
separate it from the provenance oracle (gap $0.079$ vs.\ a minimum
detectable difference of ${\approx}0.11$; \S\ref{app:power}), and on
fingerprint-free ``stealth'' pools neither defense shows measurable
erosion (\S\ref{sec:res-defense}).

\paragraph{The economics of partial failure.}
Task success did fall --- but by less than the economics deteriorated:
at $\varepsilon{=}0.75$ success lost $23$ points while regret rose
$69\%$, because most contamination losses hide \emph{inside}
``successful'' sessions as attribute mismatches and overpayment rather
than outright failures.
Success-only dashboards therefore understate the damage: an
operator sees a modest conversion dip while wasted spend, misfulfillment
costs, and refund load grow much faster. Confidence inflation compounds
this: agents err more while trusting themselves more, so downstream
automation inherits correlated, concealed losses. This pattern --- regret outrunning success,
confidence rising alongside error --- should become the standard
diagnostic pair for agents on live-content distributions, as calibration
curves preceded accuracy under distribution shift.

\paragraph{Provenance as an interface contract.}
Our defense results argue for a specific division of labor --- and
interface semantics. Content-level detection asks every
consumer to re-litigate every page, and it fails exactly on
the content that matters (near-copies of real pages). Provenance-weighted
ranking asks the supply side for one bit --- verifiable origin ---
and lets the agent treat its absence as a prior, not a verdict. The
$\lambda$-sweep adds a semantics-level finding: the bit must enter the
ranking as an \emph{ordering constraint}, not an additive bonus ---
soft integration fails for every weight within the rating scale
because manufactured 4.9-star ratings outbid it, and only recovers
once the provenance term dominates; a naive
popularity defense consuming rating$\times$review-count --- the
features the attacker manufactures --- actively amplifies the attack. An
interface contract should expose provenance as a sortable
field; platforms should treat ``provenance-blind'' signals as attack
surface. Because the bit is
cheap to spoof in the model but expensive to spoof in the world, it
converts an unbounded text-classification arms race into a bounded
key-management problem; our forgery envelope (unchanged through
$65\%$ badge forgery) shows the design tolerates display spoofing, and
the injected noise (15\% missing on real, 15\% present on synthetic) is
intentionally pessimistic --- the remaining gap to the oracle is the
price of noisy provenance, not of the idea.

\paragraph{Memory as the attack surface.}
A washout experiment adds a second-order consequence: contamination does
not only bend the choice at hand --- it leaves a trace in experience
memory, whose curation is becoming a first-class concern as
self-improving, experience-carrying agents move toward deployment
\cite{huang2026survey,fang2025webevolver}. On the discounted exam the trace decays within a few
clean episodes (half-life ${\approx}4$) and never internalizes ---
a result that holds in each of three seeds. The trace is carried by
the agent's own written notes (a write-only control shows none of it,
$p{=}0.025$), it formed under consistent
punishment (2 of 38 synthetic purchases left the agent no worse off),
and corrective feedback only directionally reduces it ($0.769$, n.s.).
A price-parity residue seen in seed-1 ($\sigma=0.842$ at clean
episode 20, above the naive agent's discounted-exam level $0.718$)
did not survive re-randomization ($p{\ge}0.46$ in both attempts).
For deployment,
the attack surface of an experience-carrying agent includes its own
note-taking: memory curation and provenance-aware filtering join
provenance-ordered ranking as defensive primitives.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our agents are 7--8B open-weight models with a scripted action interface;
frontier models with stronger browsing may behave differently, though
the channel decomposition suggests the vulnerability lives in preference
alignment rather than perceptual limits --- consistent with evidence that
verbalized confidence in LLMs is inflated \cite{zhao2026wired,wang2025calibrating}. The
mechanism decomposition and threshold law are measured on one backbone
and one generator pair; both are pre-registered, and cross-generator
replication is the next test. Industrial
farms use larger generators that could tune prices more aggressively,
but the threshold law suggests any discount suffices.
WebShop instructions are variant-level and sometimes weakly aligned
with the base listing text; official best-variant scoring makes
absolute success benchmark-specific without
interacting with the within-task randomized contamination. The
dose--response uses one item pool per task, fixed across
$\varepsilon$ (paired design); defense arms are replicated on
an independent second-generation pool; all pools and seeds are
released. ``Ambient'' is read as how content arrives --- in
the routine retrieval context, without targeting --- not whole-web
saturation; the real-document experiments
(\S\ref{sec:res-realworld}) close the self-generation gap for
clones and narrow it for reviews. Two secondary
findings are at reduced strength after replication
(\S\ref{sec:res-family}, \S\ref{sec:res-defense}); the washout
experiment is single-model, and its pre-registered
re-randomizations replicate the internalization failure in every
seed while the fast-channel carryover stays direction-consistent
but significant only in seed-1 and the parity residue does not
replicate (\S\ref{app:m1}); provenance
availability in the wild is crudely approximated by our 85/15 noise
model, and content-provenance standards themselves face desynchronization
and verification gaps \cite{golaszewski2026verifying,nemecek2026authenticated}. If agentic browsing becomes
a dominant traffic source, the question is whether machine readers can
tell what adapted: ambient contamination is the benign end of
that adaptation spectrum, deliberate manipulation the malign end, and
both flow through the same missing infrastructure --- content whose
origin a buyer can verify.
