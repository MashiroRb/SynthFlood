\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{AI-generated content on the Web.}
The scale of machine-written text on the live Web has moved from speculation to measurement: domain studies document music catalogs flooded with algorithmic tracks \cite{wu2026empirical} and a first-week field dataset of self-reported AI-generated images \cite{zewde2026gptimage}; a conceptual literature debates what ``slop'' is and why it matters \cite{kommers2025slop,nishal2026slop}, and both lay readers \cite{zhu2026humans} and automated pipelines struggle to distinguish such content. This literature treats synthetic content as a \emph{data-quality} problem for training corpora and a \emph{perception} problem for readers; we ask the downstream question it does not touch --- what happens when the consumer is an \emph{autonomous agent} that acts on it.

\paragraph{Generative engine optimization and content manipulation.}
A second line makes the production side explicit. GEO methods rewrite pages to maximize citation by generative engines \cite{wu2025what}, and a growing benchmark suite measures the arms race: E-GEO formalizes seller-side optimization in e-commerce \cite{bagga2025egeo}, GEO-Bench and its multimodal extension quantify ranking manipulation \cite{nimase2026geobench,du2026multimodal}, IF-GEO studies multi-query optimization \cite{zhou2026ifgeo}, and search-augmented LLMs are now a primary information channel \cite{miroyan2025search}, and a measurement literature quantifies brand visibility in AI search \cite{yu2026structural,chen2025generative,schulte2026dont,kumar2026generative}. Two properties shape our contribution: manipulation is almost always \emph{targeted} (pages crafted for specific queries), and the affected system is the \emph{engine} (retrieval ranking or generated answers). We study the complementary regime --- ambient, non-targeted contamination generated for commercial motives at scale --- with a \emph{decision-making agent} as the affected system. The same optimization economics powers LLM-driven shilling attacks that fabricate engagement to steer recommender rankings \cite{gu2025llmbased,qiao2025controllable,li2025semanticshield}; we study the listing side of this pressure as it reaches an autonomous buyer.

\paragraph{Agents under a polluted Web.}
Closest to us are systems that evaluate agents against corrupted information sources. The Synthetic Web builds programmatic mini-internets with adversarially curated pages and finds that language agents inherit epistemic weaknesses under single-injection falsehoods \cite{shah2026synthetic}; LLM search agents can be steered by attacker-published endorsement pages \cite{chen2026much}; a security thread demonstrates prompt-injection and memory poisoning on web agents \cite{cao2026ward,yang2026zombie,syros2026muzzle,wang2025webinject,li2025security}; persuasion studies shift agent behavior by conversational or inter-agent influence \cite{jeong2026persuasion,zhou2026epistemic}; at the retrieval layer, corpus poisoning steers dense retrieval and generation while defenses and forensics emerge \cite{li2025unsupervised,chen2025uncovering,zhang2025traceback,zhang2025benchmarking,cheng2025secure}, and cumulative AI pollution eventually collapses search quality \cite{yu2026retrieval}. Closest on the attack side, agent-security benchmarks and red-teaming studies show injected page content steering web agents into attacker-chosen actions \cite{evtimov2025wasp,liu2026pina,yan2026searchattack}. These studies measure whether an injected instruction is followed or how retrieved evidence degrades; none measures what a purchasing agent \emph{chooses} on a single results page, and none varies contamination dose. All of these attack the agent with \emph{deliberate, targeted} content or measure degradation \emph{upstream} of the decision; none asks whether \emph{ordinary} optimization pressure --- the content farms that already dominate real e-commerce --- degrades \emph{decision quality} in a dose-controlled measurement. To our knowledge, no prior work combines a forced-choice probe, a decision-level dose--response function, and regret-based economics on a grounded commerce benchmark.
