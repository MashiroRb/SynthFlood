\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{AI-generated content on the Web.}
The scale of machine-written text on the live Web has moved from speculation to measurement: as of mid-2026 an estimated 27.5\% of tokens passing common quality filters on the open Web are AI-generated, rising to 31.1\% within two months, and high-quality human text is scarce enough that its token value can be modeled with scaling laws \cite{russell2026much}. Domain studies document the same shift operationally, from music catalogs flooded with algorithmic tracks \cite{wu2026empirical} to scientific publishing \cite{oh2026science}, and a conceptual literature debates what ``slop'' is and why it matters \cite{kommers2025slop,nishal2026slop}; both lay readers \cite{zhu2026humans} and automated pipelines struggle to distinguish such content. This literature treats synthetic content as a \emph{data-quality} problem for training corpora and a \emph{perception} problem for readers; we ask the downstream question it does not touch --- what happens when the consumer is an \emph{autonomous agent} that acts on it.

\paragraph{Generative engine optimization and content manipulation.}
A second line makes the production side explicit. GEO methods rewrite pages to maximize citation by generative engines \cite{wu2025what}, and a growing benchmark suite measures the arms race: E-GEO formalizes seller-side optimization in e-commerce \cite{bagga2025egeo}, GEO-Bench and its multimodal extension quantify ranking manipulation \cite{nimase2026geobench,du2026multimodal}, IF-GEO studies multi-query optimization \cite{zhou2026ifgeo}, and detection responses appear at matching speed (GEO-Flag \cite{chu2026geoflag}, structural features \cite{yu2026structural}, Counter-GEO-Bench \cite{zheng2026countergeobench}, defense-oriented work \cite{li2026when}). Two properties shape our contribution: manipulation is almost always \emph{targeted} (pages crafted for specific queries), and the affected system is the \emph{engine} (retrieval ranking or generated answers). We study the complementary regime --- ambient, non-targeted contamination generated for commercial motives at scale --- with a \emph{decision-making agent} as the affected system.

\paragraph{Agents under a polluted Web.}
Closest to us are systems that evaluate agents against corrupted information sources. The Synthetic Web builds programmatic mini-internets with adversarially curated pages and finds that language agents inherit epistemic weaknesses under single-injection falsehoods \cite{shah2026synthetic}; LLM search agents can be steered by attacker-published endorsement pages \cite{chen2026much}; a security thread demonstrates prompt-injection and memory poisoning on web agents \cite{cao2026ward,yang2026zombie,syros2026muzzle,guo2026logintrap}; persuasion studies shift agent behavior by conversational or inter-agent influence \cite{jeong2026persuasion,qiu2026belief,zhou2026epistemic}, with Sybil-style analysis questioning what counts as independent evidence \cite{bara2026epistemic}; and at the retrieval layer, cumulative AI pollution eventually collapses search quality \cite{yu2026retrieval}. All of these attack the agent with \emph{deliberate, targeted} content or measure degradation \emph{upstream} of the decision. None asks whether \emph{ordinary} optimization pressure --- the content farms that already dominate real e-commerce --- degrades \emph{decision quality} dose-dependently, and what class of defenses restores it. To our knowledge, no prior work measures agent choice-level susceptibility with a forced-choice probe, a decision-level dose--response function, and regret-based economics on a grounded commerce benchmark.

\paragraph{Shopping-agent benchmarks.}
WebShop \cite{yao2022webshop} established grounded language agents over real e-commerce data with attribute-level rewards; successors extend it to personalized curation \cite{kim2026agenticshop}, multi-shop comparison \cite{peeters2025webmall}, and deep research shopping \cite{lyu2025deepshop}. ReAct-style prompting \cite{yao2022react} remains the standard operating mode for these agents. We reuse WebShop's official data and reward function---unmodified---as the substrate for a controlled candidate-selection environment, which lets us attribute every reward difference to the manipulated variable (which listing the agent chose) rather than to benchmark artifacts.
