Web agents increasingly buy on our behalf, while an estimated quarter of the
text they read is machine-generated under commercial optimization pressure.
Unlike adversarial injection attacks, this \emph{ambient} contamination
targets no one --- yet it may systematically distort what agents choose. We
present \textbf{SynthFlood}, a paired, dose-controlled protocol that measures
how synthetic product listings erode web-agent decision quality, built
directly on WebShop's official data and reward function. Synthetic listings
come from three families mirroring real content-farm production modes
(search-optimized pages, specification clones with altered attributes and
lower prices, review-inflated pages), generated by open-weight 7--8B models
under a cross-model plausibility floor. Three findings emerge. First, a
forced-choice probe (300 duels) shows agents buy near-correct specification
clones at chance (52\%), but controlled channel ablations show this is
\emph{not} a perceptual failure: normalizing the clone's price alone, or
stripping its inflated ratings alone, each drop the clone win-rate to 39\%;
removing both drops it to 25\% --- below the 35\% baseline for plain
gloss --- a clean two-channel decomposition. A dose--response over clone
pricing shows the same clone is \emph{rejected} at price parity (39\%) but
\emph{preferred} under any discount of 10\% or more (59\% at $-10\%$,
$p=0.003$): the attack does not fool the agent's perception, it buys the
agent's preference. Second, contamination dose degrades decisions in the
background: at $\varepsilon=0.75$, task success drops 23\,pp
($p=1.3\times10^{-4}$, replicated across seeds) while regret rises 69\%
(paired $\Delta=+0.181$) and agents become \emph{more} confident
(calibration error doubling) --- losses that no success-only metric would
surface. Third, an agent-visible badge computed from a noisy
verifiable-provenance bit eliminates the regret excess and remains
unchanged from 15\% to 65\% badge forgery; a text-free structural
detector statistically matches the perfect-provenance oracle ($p=0.21$)
--- and fingerprint-free regeneration weakens that detector only
partially ($28\%$ of its recovery) while provenance stays intact; but
the same provenance information consumed as a soft ranking bonus fails
until its weight exceeds the rating scale --- provenance must order
candidates, not score them. We
release protocol, code, contamination pools, and full trajectory logs.
These results recast ambient synthetic content as an attack on the agent's
\emph{preference function} rather than its perception, and call for
provenance-ordered ranking as a first-class interface contract between the
Web and the agents that act on it.
