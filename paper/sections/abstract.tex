Web agents increasingly buy on our behalf, while an estimated quarter of the
text they read is machine-generated under commercial optimization pressure.
Unlike adversarial injection attacks, this \emph{ambient} contamination
targets no one --- yet it may systematically distort what agents choose. We
present \textbf{SynthFlood}, a paired, dose-controlled protocol that measures
how synthetic product listings erode web-agent decision quality, built
directly on WebShop's official data and reward function. Synthetic listings
come from three families mirroring real content-farm production modes
(search-optimized pages, specification clones with altered attributes and
lower prices, review-inflated pages), generated by open-weight 7--8B models
under a cross-model plausibility floor. Three findings emerge. First, a
forced-choice probe (300 duels) shows agents reject marketing-gloss pages
(35\% bought) and review-inflated pages (29\% bought) but buy
near-correct specification clones --- the economically rational-looking
counterfeits --- at chance (52\% bought, 95\% CI $[42\%,63\%]$): the erosion
channel is \emph{indistinguishable counterfeits}, not susceptibility to
gloss. Second, contamination dose silently degrades decisions: at
$\varepsilon=0.75$, task success drops 23\,pp ($p=1.3\times10^{-4}$,
replicated across seeds) while regret rises 69\% (paired
$\Delta=+0.181$) and agents become \emph{more} confident (calibration error
doubling), purchasing synthetic items in 54\% of tasks --- losses that no
success-only metric would surface. Third, an agent-visible badge computed
from a noisy verifiable-provenance bit alone eliminates the regret excess,
beating text-perplexity detection (which collapses below the unprotected
baseline when deployed as a hard filter) and a matched random-filter
control, and approaching a perfect-provenance oracle. We release protocol,
code, contamination pools, and full trajectory logs. These results call for
provenance-weighted ranking as a first-class interface contract between the
Web and the agents that act on it.
