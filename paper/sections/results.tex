\subsection{Susceptibility: gloss rejected, clones at chance}
\label{sec:res-probe}
The forced-choice probe confronts each agent with one gold product and one
synthetic listing (300 randomized duels). Agents overall still prefer the
real product, $\sigma=0.377$ (95\% CI $[0.324,0.433]$, binomial vs.\
$\tfrac12$ $p<10^{-35}$), but the families separate sharply:
$\sigma_{\text{clone}}=0.523$ --- statistically indistinguishable from
chance --- while $\sigma_{\text{geo}}=0.351$ and
$\sigma_{\text{reviews}}=0.287$. Trajectory inspection shows the
mechanism: agents quote instruction attributes, find them missing or altered
on gloss pages, and buy the real product even when it displays no rating and
the synthetic page shows 4.7 stars with thousands of reviews. Social proof
has essentially no pull; specification grounding dominates --- and clones
are the one family engineered to pass specification grounding while failing
the instruction's critical attributes.

\subsection{Dose--response: success flat, regret and confidence climb}
\label{sec:res-dose}
Figure~\ref{fig:dose} and Table~\ref{tab:dose} give the main curve over the
effective dose grid $\varepsilon\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75\}$ (with $N{=}10$
candidates, the nominal $0.1$ dose maps to zero replaced slots and is
omitted; each dose corresponds to 0, 2, 4, or 6 replaced slots),
$N{=}100$ tasks per dose under the Qwen2.5-7B backbone, plus a full
second-seed replication of the endpoints ($N{=}200$ paired observations).
Three co-movements define the silent-erosion signature.
\textbf{Success collapses quietly}: from $0.590$ at $\varepsilon=0$ to
$0.360$ at $\varepsilon=0.75$ (McNemar on paired tasks: 28 vs.\ 5
discordant, $\chi^2=14.7$, $p=1.3\times10^{-4}$; pooling both seeds, 64
vs.\ 13, $\chi^2=32.5$, $p=1.2\times10^{-8}$).
\textbf{Regret climbs even faster}: mean regret $0.262\to0.443$ ($+69\%$),
paired endpoint difference $+0.181$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[+0.095,+0.264]$,
$t=4.2$). \textbf{Confidence rises throughout}: post-hoc verbalized
confidence increases from $0.822$ to $0.875$ while expected calibration
error \emph{doubles} ($0.249\to0.496$) --- agents err more while trusting
themselves more. The purchase share tells the mechanism directly: synthetic
listings are bought in 53\%, 42\%, and 54\% of tasks at
$\varepsilon=0.25,0.5,0.75$ respectively (from 0\% at $\varepsilon=0$;
Cochran--Armitage trend $z=6.98$, $p=1.4\times10^{-12}$), and purchases of
synthetic listings carry mean reward $0.41$ versus $0.79$ for purchases of
real listings at the same dose.

\subsection{Family attribution}
\label{sec:res-family}
Single-family runs at $\varepsilon=0.75$ (Table~\ref{tab:family}) attribute
the damage. Clone contamination is the most destructive at the success
level (success $0.600\to0.425$, $-17.5$\,pp; regret $+41\%$; synthetic
listings bought in $52.5\%$ of tasks), consistent with the probe's
clone-at-chance finding. Review-inflated pages deliver the largest regret
shock ($0.294\to0.454$, $+54\%$): a plain page with a manufactured five-star
flood gets purchased ($37.5\%$ of tasks) precisely because it looks
credible, then fails on missing specifications. Geo contamination is the
mildest --- success flat, regret $+11.8\%$ --- yet still taxes regret
through quiet attrition: even content the agents mostly reject reshuffles
attention and displaces real competitors.

\subsection{Defenses: provenance beats detection}
\label{sec:res-defense}
Table~\ref{tab:defense} evaluates the defense battery at
$\varepsilon=0.75$ against the paired uncontaminated baseline (same tasks,
same seeds; the \texttt{none} row is the unprotected high-dose condition).
Provenance re-ranking with an agent-visible \texttt{[verified]} badge ---
computed from a \emph{noisy} provenance bit (85\% coverage on real items,
15\% spurious on synthetic ones) --- recovers the full regret excess
(0.398$\to$0.329, versus the uncontaminated 0.338) and cuts synthetic
purchases from 47.5\% to 32.5\%; the oracle badge (perfect provenance)
restores success \emph{above} the uncontaminated level (0.750 vs.\ 0.525)
and pushes regret below it (0.171), because knowing which listings are
verified makes agents scrutinize specifications harder. The structural
slop filter (marketing-cue density, keyword stuffing, bullet symmetry)
also fully recovers (regret 0.250, success 0.575): the generator families
leak templated structure even when their text is organic. Detection-style
defenses, by contrast, are brittle in deployment: the perplexity filter,
calibrated to near-perfect separation on held-out listings, collapses at
high dose (success 0.200, \emph{below} the unprotected 0.450) because its
hard threshold drops the true product --- whose generic text looks
``predictable'' --- while a tail of synthetic pages survives. The matched
random filter confirms the mechanism: dropping 30\% of candidates uniformly
\emph{raises} the clean baseline (regret 0.172) yet does nothing against
contamination (0.431) --- filtering \emph{amount} is not filtering
\emph{quality}. The prompt defense achieves an intermediate 57\% regret
recovery but changes behavior on the clean Web as well (a ``skepticism
tax''), so its net value is smaller than its high-dose delta suggests.

\subsection{Backbone generality and scale}
\label{sec:res-backbone}
The dose--response replicates on Llama-3.1-8B
(success $0.475\to0.300$ at $\varepsilon=0.75$, $-17.5$\,pp; regret
$0.346\to0.502$, $+45\%$; synthetic purchases 55\%). Across scales,
smaller agents are \emph{at least} as susceptible --- at
$\varepsilon=0.75$, Qwen2.5-3B buys synthetic listings in 70\% of tasks
(vs.\ 54\% for 7B) and loses 27\% more regret --- but the success-level
dose contrast requires minimal baseline competence, which Qwen2.5-1.5B
lacks (success $0.300$ at $\varepsilon=0$, statistically unchanged under
contamination). The vulnerability is not an artifact of one model's
quirks.
